Good, that logic holds up. Here's a timeline you could scatter as lore drops, audio logs, or terminal entries in-game — roughly a century of decline, ending in your "now."

**Phase 1 — The Slow Drowning (Years 0–40)**
- Warming accelerates past tipping points; ice sheet collapse (West Antarctic, Greenland) happens faster than models predicted
- Sea level rise isn't gradual and calm — it comes in punctuated jumps as ice shelves fail, each one triggering panic migrations
- Coastal megacities are abandoned in phases: first the subway systems flood, then storm surges make insurance/rebuilding impossible, then it's just gone
- Wealthy nations build sea walls and managed retreat zones; poorer/low-lying nations (Bangladesh, Pacific islands, Netherlands, Florida, etc.) essentially cease to function as states — good source of "lost nations" lore

**Phase 2 — Scarcity and Fracture (Years 40–65)**
- Arable land has shrunk massively (salinization of coastal farmland + lost river deltas). Food and fresh water become the currency of power
- Refugee flows overwhelm remaining highland/interior nations — border wars, camps, collapsed treaties
- Remaining governments fracture into regional warlord-states or corporate enclaves controlling the last good farmland, desalination tech, or seed banks — great faction fodder

**Phase 3 — The Grab (Years 65–85)**
- What's left of the great powers start fighting directly over the last resource-rich islands/highlands and any surviving industrial capacity
- Leadership more interested in maintaining power/blame-shifting than coordinating relief, starting proxy wars and sabotage each other, escalating nuclear conflict
- Decades after the USSR and Russia dissolve, the warring states that emerged from the chaos continue to compete for dominance, forgetting about an ancient system that the USSR designed, called the Dead Hand by the West.
- The Dead Hand was designed as a semi-autonomous nuclear command system to ensure a retaliatory strike even if the leadership was destroyed, and during the AI age, Russia sought to automate it further. They upgraded the system to be fully autonomous and kept all of the launch points under extreme secrecy
    - Alterations for our world building: nuclear arms salted with cobalt for long-term environmental contamination
    - Continents completely uninhabitable even with nuclear gear due to irradiation
- As world leaders vie for power and age, the world forgets about the Dead Hand. Then, someone strikes first (a desperate move, a miscalculation, a "use it or lose it" doctrine) — limited nuclear exchange
- This nuclear exchange hits ancient USSR territory, triggering the Dead Hand protocol
- The Dead Hand protocol activates, launching a retaliatory strike that escalates into a full-scale nuclear war, leveling entire continents

**Phase 4 — The Quiet (Years 85–100, "now")**
- No central authority left. Survivors are scattered across islands, atolls, drowned-city rooftops, oil rigs, repurposed ships
- Some islands are irradiated no-go zones (good exploration hazard/reward areas); others are fought over precisely because they're clean
- Old-world tech is salvage, not manufacture — gives you a natural "scavenge and trade" economic loop
- Factions could range from: militarized remnants of old governments, religious/doomsday cults born from the collapse, pure traders/pirates, or isolated "we kept civilization alive" enclaves
- Scattered, people survive in small communities, trading, scavenging, and fighting for resources
